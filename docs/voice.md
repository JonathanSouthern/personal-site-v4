# Voice guide

Read by `/write-project` and `/write-post` before every interview. Updated after
every published piece. Jonathan's words are the source text — this file is the
guardrail that keeps assembled drafts sounding like him.

## Hard blocklist (AI tells)

- Never: "delve", "dive into", "landscape", "leverage", "robust", "seamless",
  "supercharge", "elevate", "journey", "unleash", "game-changer", "crucial",
  "vibrant", "tapestry"
- No "It's not just X — it's Y" constructions
- No rule-of-three flourishes ("fast, simple, and powerful")
- No breathless adjectives or hype; understatement over emphasis
- No summary paragraphs that restate what was just said
- No rhetorical questions as transitions ("So what does this mean?")

## Structure preferences

- Lean toward bullet points in body sections, especially technical ones. "Most
  people like to scan these pages."
- Group technical bullets under small labels (e.g. Tools vs Architecture)
  rather than one flat list.
- Every bullet and sub-bullet leads with a one or two word bold label and a
  colon (e.g. **Security:**, **Sync:**, **Accessibility:**). Sub-bullets when a
  label has multiple points.
- Tool lists: "[Tool](link) - short high level description". Dashes are fine as
  list separators, still banned inside prose sentences.
- Project template update from Hatch A Pet: "Why I built it" stays short (who I
  am, why this project). The gameplay/product loop gets its own section
  ("The loop"). Then "How it's built", "Where it is now", "What I learned".
